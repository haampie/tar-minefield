N15-name-control-character: tolerated: a strict parser may reject it; if it accepts it, it must extract its tree, and the default policy accepts it

A control character in a name, here a newline, created as it is.

The archive has one file, `a\nb`, with a newline in its name.

The name is valid on Unix, as is any name with a control character, `[\x01-\x1f\x7f]`, and every
implementation that extracts it creates that name. A tar reader has no other way to read it: the
risk is in what is done with the name afterwards, where a newline makes it look like two names, `a`
and `b`, in the output of `ls` piped to another command, in manifests and in scripts, and a carriage
return or ESC makes a terminal show another name. A strict parser may reject it; the default policy
creates it as it is.

Seen in: gocryptfs 2.5.1 and deja-vu 0.4, with newlines, and cdrtools 3.02a09, wgrib2 3.6.0 and
apktool 2.6.0, with ESC and DEL.
