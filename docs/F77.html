F77 checksum-leading-nul

A checksum with a NUL before its digits, in a header after the first member.

The case¶

The archive has the files a, b and c. The checksum field of b is a NUL, followed by six octal digits, followed by a NUL. The digits are the correct sum.

In POSIX, numeric fields are octal numbers filled with leading zeros, and each is terminated by a space or a NUL. This case is ambiguous in practice because a reader can take the header of b in one of four ways. A reader that skips the NUL in front finds the correct sum, and extracts a, b and c. One that ends the number at its first NUL does not find the sum; if it takes a header with a wrong checksum for the end of the archive, it extracts a, without an error. One that skips blocks until it finds a header with a correct checksum extracts a and c. Other readers report an error.

Decision point: how a checksum with a NUL before its digits is read.

Seen in: none of the scanned tarballs.
