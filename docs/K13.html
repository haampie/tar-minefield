The case¶

The archive has a file a, whose mode field is 755 in base-256: the byte 0x80, followed by the number in binary.

In GNU's format, a number is written in base-256 when it does not fit in the octal digits of its field, as a large size or uid does not. A mode has 12 bits, which always fit, so writers do not have a reason to write it in base-256, and not every reader of base-256 sizes reads a mode in it. This case is ambiguous in practice because a reader can take the field in one of three ways. A reader that reads base-256 in every numeric field extracts a as an executable file. One that reads base-256 only in some fields does not find octal digits in this one, and extracts a without the execute permission. Other readers report an error.

Decision point: whether a mode may be in base-256.

Seen in: none of the scanned tarballs.
